"I was a kid and nobody knew how to spell it."

That’s Oprah Winfrey, 72, explaining why the world knows her as Oprah when her actual birth name was Orpah Gail Winfrey—a name pulled straight from the Bible’s Book of Ruth, where Orpah is the sister-in-law of the titular convert.

The revelation came during a chat on Keke Palmer’s podcast, *Baby, This Is Keke Palmer*, where Winfrey casually dropped the truth bomb that even she didn’t realize her name had been mispronounced and reshaped for most of her life. "I didn't learn until I was probably 20 or something," she admitted, "that, 'Oh, this isn't really my name.'" Her birth certificate, as confirmed by E! News, still reads *Orpah*.

## What Was Said

Palmer, host of the podcast and star of *We Love Boosters*, didn’t miss a beat. Joking in response, she said, "Wow, we have so much in common, because I'm actually 'Eekey.'" Then, laughing, added, "I'm just bein' stupid," before revealing her real name: Lauren Keyana Palmer.

Two women, two stage names, one shared understanding: fame often demands a rebrand. Sometimes it’s strategic. Sometimes it’s just survival.

Winfrey’s case wasn’t a calculated move—it was a linguistic slip that stuck. Mispronounced so often as a child, *Orpah* morphed into *Oprah*, and by the time she realized the difference, the name had already taken on a life of its own. No legal change, no announcement—just a decades-long autocorrect by the world.

She’s far from alone. Paul McCartney, for instance, isn’t technically *Paul*—his full name is James Paul McCartney. As he shared on his website in 2023, his parents used his middle name to avoid confusion with his father, James McCartney. The tradition skipped a beat but returned with his son, James Louis McCartney.

Then there’s Reese Witherspoon. Born Laura Jeanne Reese Witherspoon, she adopted her mother’s maiden name—Reese—when she entered Hollywood. As she reminded Nicole Kidman in a *Vanity Fair* interview, it was partly to avoid name clashes. "We can't both be Laura," she said, referring to *Big Little Lies* costar Laura Dern.

Michael Keaton’s story is a classic Hollywood workaround. Born Michael John Douglas, he had to change his name because another actor—Michael Douglas—was already registered with the Screen Actors Guild. How’d he land on *Keaton*? "I must’ve gone, ‘I don’t know, let me think of something here.’ And I went, ‘Oh, that sounds reasonable.’"

Others followed suit for different reasons. Jamie Foxx, born Eric Marlon Bishop, chose a gender-neutral stage name to improve his odds of getting stage time early in his comedy career. Post Malone, born Austin Richard Post, kept his surname but flipped it into a brand—though close friends still call him Austin.

Even younger stars have trimmed their names for simplicity. Aubrey Frances Anderson-Emmons, known for *Modern Family*, now goes by Frances Anderson—her middle name, named for her mother’s old friend. The full hyphenated version, she’s said, was just too long.

Dakota and Elle Fanning? Born Mary and Hannah Fanning, respectively. They swapped in their middle names when entering the industry. As Dakota once said, "There’s no Mary here."

And yes, even reality TV isn’t immune. William Carl Radke, a cast member on *Summer House* for all nine seasons, goes by Carl—his middle name—despite his legal first name being William. His passport and ID confirm it.

Sterling K. Brown, born Sterling Kelby Brown, used his middle name until his teens. His father? Sterling Brown Jr. His grandfather? Sterling Brown Sr. So the “K” wasn’t just flair—it was necessity.
