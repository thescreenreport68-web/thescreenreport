The [Marvel](/movies/nobody-was-ready-for-that-final-spider-man-brand-new-day-trailer/) Cinematic Universe has its new Black Panther. The move to pass the mantle sent fans into a frenzy, with reactions ranging from enthusiastic celebration to skeptical disappointment.

## A Wave of Optimism and Trust

For a large contingent of fans, the news is a cause for celebration. Many point directly to Jonsson's recent performances as proof he's up to the task, expressing full faith in the creative team behind the film.

One user wrote, "His performances in Alien and The Long Walk led to this🎉 now he in the MCU.. his career really abt to take off🔥"

Another viewer posted, "Extremely into the idea of David Jonsson starring in a Ryan Coogler movie! That's all I need to know!"

The sentiment is that Jonsson is a talented actor who can bring the necessary gravitas to Wakanda's throne. One fan, referencing his work in *The Long Walk*, said, "After seeing him in the long walk im sure the dude will nail it he was able convince me that he could inspire bravery in dire situations perfect quality for a king"

This trust extends beyond just the actor to the director and the overall legacy. A fan online said, "BP2 was pretty much as good as it could have been given the circumstances and choices made following the tragic death of Chadwick Boseman. I have full faith BP3 will shine. David Jonsson is an amazing casting choice."

## The Skeptics and Alternative Casting Calls

Not everyone was ready to embrace the announcement. A vocal segment of the audience expressed immediate disappointment, often citing specific actors they felt were better suited for the iconic role.

One common name was Aldis Hodge. A viewer posted, "Aldis Hodge would’ve been perfect! He gave Black Panther vibes in Black Adam"

Another echoed, "Honestly, Aldis Hodge all the way. He looks like *T'Challa*"

Damson Idris was another popular fan choice. One person noted, "I can't believe David Jonsson beat out Damson! I trust Marvel casting though. For this. Not everything."

Beyond specific actors, some questioned the timing of the decision itself. One user wrote, "Ngl they really should've done this in wakanda forever"

## The Cautious Middle Ground

Perhaps the most common reaction fell into a pragmatic, wait-and-see category. This group is urging patience, emphasizing that a performance, not a press release, is what truly matters.

A fan online said, "we'll have to see how he does we just have to give him a chance."

Another viewer posted a blunt strategy: "wait and see...destroy them after, if they deserve it. Praise if they deserve it."

This sentiment reflects an audience that has been through the wringer with superhero casting before and knows that the final product is the only thing that counts.

## The Unacceptable Fringe

In the uglier corner of the reaction, a handful of posts veered into outright bigotry, a disheartening but predictable element of any major franchise casting. One post, which suggested, "why not a white black panther like helen of troy ?" stands as a stark example of the racist undercurrent that sometimes surfaces in these discussions. It’s a reminder that for all the legitimate debate about acting chops and screen presence, there remains a fringe that fundamentally misunderstands or opposes the core identity of a character like the Black Panther.

## Why the Reaction Is So Split

The division makes sense. Casting the new Black Panther isn't just filling a role; it's stewarding a legacy left by Chadwick Boseman and a symbol of immense cultural significance. For some, Jonsson represents a fresh, exciting talent vetted by a trusted director like Ryan Coogler. For others, their mental image of T'Challa's successor had already been cast with a different face. The David Jonsson [Black Panther 3 casting](/movies/winston-duke-confirms-return-for-black-panther-3-david-jonsson-joins-cast/) reaction is ultimately a testament to how personally invested audiences are in this character and this world. They care deeply, and that care manifests as both fierce support and sharp criticism.

## Where the Conversation Heads Next

The discourse is now set on a path toward December 2028. Jonsson's own humble statement—"Thank you to this amazing family that I have the honor and privilege and the blessing to join"—and the enthusiastic backing from his future co-star Letitia Wright, who joked, "I’m going to pick on him so much," will help solidify support among the optimistic. The skeptical will be watching for the first trailer, the first still, any sign of how Jonsson embodies the mantle. For now, the internet has rendered its first, fractured verdict. The real judgment, as the cautious middle knows, is still four years away.
