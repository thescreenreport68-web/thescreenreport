For over a decade, it was the ultimate [Star Wars](/movies/a-star-wars-powerwash-pack-has-fans-in-a-chokehold-but-not-for-the-reason-you/) "what if." Now, the long-dormant animated parody *Star Wars Detours* is finally escaping the vault.

## The Moment the Timeline Exploded

The news broke simply: *Star Wars Detours*, [George Lucas](/movies/george-lucas-says-ai-is-the-future-and-fans-shouldn-t-control-movies-the/)'s final, unreleased Star Wars project, would be screened. For fans who had followed the project's development and subsequent shelving in 2013, it felt like unearthing buried treasure. The immediate reaction was stunned excitement.

The all-caps declaration captured the feeling perfectly. This wasn't just another re-release; it was the unveiling of a piece of Star Wars history many thought they'd never see. For this segment of the audience, the Lucas Museum's move was a cause for celebration, a validation of years of curiosity.

In this context, *Detours*—an animated parody once destined for television—fits perfectly. It represents a commercial, mass-audience project from the mind of George Lucas himself, now being presented as a piece of cultural history worthy of museum study.

For the creators involved, the news is profoundly emotional. Seth Green, who developed the series, described the original experience of making it in glowing terms. "It was the greatest. It was literally one of the greatest experiences I think I’ve ever had," Green said. When asked about the screening, he simply stated, "It’s kind of extraordinary." His joy is palpable and shared by many fans who see this as a rightful conclusion to a long, uncertain journey for the project.

## Not Everyone Is Celebrating

However, the announcement also activated a more critical minority. Their concerns weren't necessarily about *Detours* itself, but about the museum's broader curatorial direction. The museum's mission centers on "narrative art," and for some, the inclusion of experimental film feels like a detour.

One critic posted a pointed critique: "Not sure I like the Slavko Vorkapich Theater theme. It is off mission of storytelling with experimental film. I like a film as an a art form the it is not 'narrative art'. I hoped that film program would focus on pioneer (Edison, Méliès) story"

This viewer draws a clear line between experimental film and narrative storytelling, expressing disappointment that the museum isn't focusing more squarely on the pioneers of cinematic narrative. This skepticism extends to *Detours* itself for some. Another fan expressed complete indifference, writing, "I would probably stand in line for tickets 30 years ago before Star Wars prequel happened….now? Pass! I wouldn’t go even if it’s free."

This sentiment suggests that for a segment of the audience, the project feels dated, a relic of a different era in Star Wars history that no longer holds appeal. It's a reminder that time can alter anticipation, and not every lost project is viewed as a hidden gem.

## What's Next for the Star Wars Detours Lucas Museum Event?

The screening is set to be a centerpiece of the museum's presence at [Comic-Con](/movies/san-diego-comic-con-2026-a-celebration-of-comics-or-a-digital-pop-culture/), sharing space with programs honoring artistic pioneers like Frank Frazetta and Michael Whelan. The reaction to those announcements was also positive, with one artist noting, "Frazetta & Michael Whelan clips! My mentors from afar :)." Another educator commented, "Excellent! I'm happy to see focus on experimental works. I'll share with my film students."

This highlights the dual nature of the response: overwhelming excitement from specific communities of artists, filmmakers, and dedicated fans, met with philosophical debate about the museum's core identity. The event will ultimately serve as a test case. Can a broad, museum-going audience engage with a shelved TV parody as "narrative art"? Will the joy of finally seeing *Detours* outweigh critiques of its relevance or the museum's programming choices?

For now, the consensus is one of historic celebration tempered by thoughtful debate. The vault is open, and after years of waiting, fans will finally get to judge George Lucas's final Star Wars curio for themselves.
