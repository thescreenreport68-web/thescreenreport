Mike Flanagan's upcoming Prime Video series '[Carrie](/tv/prime-video-debuts-first-look-at-mike-flanagan-s-carrie-series/)' just dropped its first trailer, and the reaction was instant — and loud. The horror mastermind behind 'The Haunting of Hill House' is promising a fresh take, but the audience is already choosing sides between those thrilled by the update and those who've seen enough prom nights.

## A Carrie for the digital age
For a lot of viewers, the big draw is the promise of a deeper, more contemporary look at bullying. The trailer's focus on digital-age cruelty hit home, especially one line that really resonated. One viewer posted, "'And the worst part... is that I really could've been your friend' 😭😭🥺,", capturing the story's tragic heart for a new generation. This vibe was everywhere, with another fan writing, "Knowing the story and hearing 'I really could have been your friend' made me kind of depressed. I’m really excited to see what Mike Flanagan does with this.".

This excitement extends to how Carrie herself is being portrayed. Fans are noticing a shift from the more isolated versions we've seen before. "Like how they do things differently by actually having her socialize with others, when the previous versions of her literally never hung out with anyone,", one user observed. "Having the girls pretend to be her friends makes it even more tragic.". There's also huge anticipation for the scale of that infamous finale, with viewers pointing to specific trailer moments: "0:43 the whole town! They're actually gonna have her wreck the whole town this time! 🔥" and "0:44 Looks like we'll be finally getting the town destruction which has only been seen in the 2002 made for tv movie.".

## Is this remake really necessary?
On the other side, a significant chunk of the reaction is pure skepticism. The fact that we keep getting new 'Carrie' adaptations was a common complaint. "They make this movie every 15 years it's like the spiderman of horror movies,", one user noted dryly. That weariness is mixed with a long-running gripe about all 'Carrie' adaptations: they keep casting conventionally attractive actresses in the role.

The demand for a Carrie that actually looks like the book description was a major talking point. "Carrie is described as being overweight and covered in pimples, it’s literally important to the plot because they do the 'pigs blood for a pig' and her appearance is why she is bullied,", one fan argued pointedly. This critique kept popping up, with others stating, "Carrie in the book was actually quite overweight, but they always ignore that in all adaptations," and simply lamenting, "One of these days we’ll get a book accurate Carrie.".

## Flanagan's case for a new prom night
Flanagan and his team know they have to win people over. In interviews, Flanagan has directly addressed the legendary status of Brian De Palma's 1976 film, calling it "untouchable.". He says the reason for a new version is how much the world has changed. "The world has changed quite a lot,", he said, pointing a finger right at modern cruelty. "[We will dive into] the cruelty in today’s world and how the internet has encouraged cruelty, rewards and celebrates and amplifies it. This is awful for adults to deal with, and for teenagers even more so.".

This plan to dig into bullying in the smartphone era is the show's big idea, aiming to make Carrie White's tragedy feel urgent all over again. Star Summer Howell described her Carrie as someone who "goes into this very open and excited for the world,", which lines up with the fan buzz about a more socially connected protagonist. Flanagan has also teased that this adaptation will surprise people, stating, "I think a lot of people think they know what the show is. And the biggest thing I’m excited about today is just knowing what a big surprise our Carrie is going to be.".

Despite that vision, the negative reaction, while smaller, was sharp. Some were totally over the whole idea, with one post declaring, "Just remembered that Mike Flanagan has the pen on the next adaptation of Carrie, and wow is that going to be a monstrosity,", while another bluntly asked, "do we have to remake this every 10 years.....".

The discourse isn't cooling off any time soon. This is a classic fandom split: between the hope for smart innovation from a trusted creator and sheer fatigue with a story we've seen before. Whether Flanagan's modern lens and deeper character work can win over the skeptics is the big question, and we'll get the answer when all eight episodes hit Prime Video this October.
