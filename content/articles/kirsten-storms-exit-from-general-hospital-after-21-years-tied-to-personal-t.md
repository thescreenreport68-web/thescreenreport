Kirsten Storms’ character, Maxie Jones, was officially written off 'General Hospital' in a Thursday episode, relocating to Australia just months after waking from a coma. The move marks the end of her 21-year run on the soap opera. She previously took a six-month hiatus from July 2025 to February 2026, during which time she told her followers she planned to undergo brain surgery. She was only featured in a small batch of episodes in the months leading up to her most recent exit.

## What Was Said

In June, Storms announced she would be taking another break from the series, citing her ongoing legal issues with ex-husband Brandon Barash. In a post on Instagram, she shared her hesitation to return to Los Angeles, writing: "Given what’s been going on, I can’t say I will feel comfortable heading back to Los Angeles and returning to work. This has been an eye opening experience about the downside of my profession."

That statement offered a rare window into her state of mind.

The post came amid escalating tensions with Barash, whom she met on the set of 'General Hospital' and married in 2013. The couple divorced in 2016. Since then, their co-parenting relationship has been strained, according to court filings reported by Page Six.

In April, Barash was granted an emergency restraining order against Storms after telling a judge she was experiencing a "mental health crisis." He expressed concern for the safety of their 12-year-old daughter, Harper, alleging that Storms had been experiencing delusions and hallucinations.

## How We Got Here

He alleged she changed hotel rooms repeatedly, believing people were 'following her and the child to her room.'

During this period, she faced multiple financial struggles, including a lawsuit for eviction and the risk of car repossession. Barash claimed he loaned her $5,000 to prevent the repossession.

Later that month, Storms was hospitalized on a psychiatric hold after Barash reported her behavior to authorities. She was released after evaluation, but a friend later told Page Six she turned to abusing Adderall and potentially other substances after her release.

Barash asked the court to compel Storms to undergo a mental health evaluation, arguing that her condition posed a risk to their daughter.

Despite the show’s rep not responding to Page Six’s request for comment, the narrative on screen moved swiftly. Maxie Jones, who had just emerged from a coma in the same episode she was written off, was suddenly relocating to Australia — a tidy, off-screen solution to an actor’s real-world unavailability.

The storyline shift was abrupt, even by soap opera standards. Fans had barely welcomed Maxie back before she was gone again. But the speed of the exit aligns with Storms’ own admission that she didn’t feel safe returning to Los Angeles.

It’s a rare moment when a character’s fate so clearly echoes the actor’s personal crisis. While soap operas often write characters out with amnesia, secret twins, or sudden inheritances, this departure feels less like fiction and more like accommodation.

The move to Australia was announced on the episode of 'General Hospital,' wrapping Maxie’s arc with minimal fanfare. No grand farewell, no final confrontation — just a quiet exit, much like the way Storms herself has stepped back from the public eye.

This is not the first time Storms has stepped away from the role. This latest break, however, appears more complex, entangled in legal, emotional, and familial challenges.

Over 21 years, she’s weathered love triangles, betrayals, and even death — only to be written out now, not by plot, but by circumstance.
