The [Marvel](/movies/avengers-doomsday-fans-are-hyped-critics-are-skeptical/) Cinematic Universe is about to get a whole lot more fiery — and the internet went into full meltdown the moment the news dropped. While some are celebrating what they see as a perfect piece of casting, others are left feeling burned by the decision to move past a beloved previous incarnation.

## The Hype Train Has No Brakes

For a vocal segment of the fandom, the news is an absolute victory. The prospect of [Ryan Gosling](/movies/how-project-hail-mary-sent-fans-into-a-frenzy/), with his unique blend of brooding charisma and raw star power, stepping into the role of the supernatural antihero has been a long-held fantasy. The excitement isn't just about the actor; it's about the doors this opens for the future of the MCU's darker corners.

One user wrote, "Ryan Gosling as Ghost Rider is for sure FIRE 🔥🔥🔥"

A fan online posted, "Oh hell yeah. Midnight Sons could be amazing with Gosling as Ghost Rider, Mahershala Ali as Blade and Oscar Isaac and Moon Knight. That's a helluva leading trio right there."

For others, the casting feels almost too good to be true. Another viewer said, "Ryan as Ghost Rider is something I only could've dreamed of in some sort of la la land, i prayed hail marys for this casting cant wait to see him drive around on that bike" This sentiment captures the sheer, joyful disbelief many are feeling, viewing Gosling's involvement as a sign that Marvel is pulling out all the stops to reinvigorate the franchise. The simple, ecstatic reaction of "Yay. Ghostrider my favorite character. But Ryan Gosling as Ghostrider is a dream come true" sums up this side of the debate perfectly.

## Not Everyone Is Buying It

Not everyone is celebrating. For a passionate segment of fans, particularly those who followed Marvel's television endeavors, this announcement feels like a step backward. The most pointed criticism centers on the decision to cast a new Johnny Blaze (or Dan Ketch) instead of bringing back Gabriel Luna's Robbie Reyes from *Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D.*, a version that was widely praised for its fresh take and cultural significance.

One user posted bluntly, "All AoS fans found crying in the corner at Ghost Rider not being Robbie Reyes"

The disappointment goes beyond simple preference, touching on issues of representation. A fan online argued, "The fact that they had an amazing Ghost Rider in Gabriel Luna for Agents of Shield and they’re going with a white guy also sorta reinforces that whole vibe." This critique highlights a deeper frustration with Marvel's approach to legacy and diversity, where a well-established, Latino incarnation of the hero is seemingly passed over for a new, high-profile star.

Mixed in with this specific disappointment is a broader skepticism about the character's viability on the big screen. Another viewer expressed doubt, writing, "I really enjoyed Ghost Rider as a comic when I was a kid but I still don't know how you'd successfully make a compelling Ghost Rider movie that wasn't entirely reliant on fans of the character carrying it (which is impossible)" This points to a core challenge: can Ghost Rider, a niche and visually extreme character, anchor a blockbuster for general audiences?

Some fans try to find a middle ground, acknowledging the loss while hoping for a nod to the past. One person suggested, "I'm disappointed it's not Gabriel Luna's Robbie Reyes from Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D. but I'm glad it's not Nicolas Cage's Ghost Rider 😅 The fact that it's a new Rider tells me that they could hint that the previous Rider was AOS's Robbie Reyes through dialog,"

## The Shawn Levy Factor: Hype or Halt?

Adding another layer to the divided Ryan Gosling Ghost Rider MCU fan reactions is the choice of director, Shawn Levy. While Levy's collaboration with Gosling on *The Adam Project* and the upcoming *The Fall Guy* suggests a strong working relationship, his filmography, heavy on family-friendly adventure and comedy, gives some fans pause for a character known for hellfire and vengeance.

The mixed feelings are captured perfectly in one user's rollercoaster post: "LFG Ryan Gosling is going to be an amazing Ghost Rider! Finally the adaptation I wanted! Wait, what? I sorry... WHO is DIRECTING? FFS"

The studio's confidence, however, seems unshaken. In a statement, director Shawn Levy said, "Gosling is the absolute shit." He added, "I say that with tremendous respect." Marvel Studios president [Kevin Feige](/movies/kevin-feige-s-adam-driver-joke-masks-a-deeper-fan-backlash-over-marvel-s-missed/)'s reported comment, "Hey man, if Ryan wants to be Ghost Rider…" also suggests this was a casting driven by star passion as much as studio strategy.

## The Great Ghost Rider Divide

The division stems from a collision of several powerful fan currents. On one side is the sheer gravitational pull of a major movie star like Gosling joining a beloved universe—it feels like an event. On the other is the deep loyalty fans have to the expansive Marvel storytelling that happened on television, which often feels sidelined by the cinematic arm. Furthermore, Ghost Rider himself occupies a unique space: he's iconic visually but has a checkered film history, leaving fans both hungry for a great adaptation and nervous it might misfire.

The conversation is now racing in two directions. Optimists are busy fantasy-casting Midnight Sons rosters and debating which Ghost Rider lineage Gosling will inhabit. Skeptics and disappointed fans are voicing their concerns louder, hoping Marvel might still find a way to integrate the legacy of Robbie Reyes. What's clear is that Marvel has successfully captured everyone's attention, but they'll have to navigate a minefield of sky-high expectations and passionate legacy concerns to make this iteration of the Spirit of Vengeance a success.
