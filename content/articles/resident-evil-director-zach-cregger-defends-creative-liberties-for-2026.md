Director Zach Cregger defended his creative liberties with the upcoming '[Resident Evil](/movies/new-resident-evil-movie-trailer-promises-faithful-fan-focused-horror/)' film adaptation during a panel at Comic-Con in July 2026. The writer-director, known for the hit film 'Weapons', explained his decision to modernize the setting and forge his own path within the iconic video game universe.

Cregger's film, set for release on September 18, 2026, is a new cinematic take on Capcom's survival horror franchise. It follows Bryan, a medical courier played by Austin Abrams, who is delivering a package to Raccoon City when a viral outbreak plunges the city into chaos.

## What Changes Did Cregger Make?

The director was direct about his deviations from the 1998 game 'Resident Evil 2', which serves as a loose inspiration. He moved the story from the 1990s to the present day and added a snowy setting, choices he knew would draw scrutiny from purists.

"It takes place in the snow in 2026. That’s why [Austin Abrams’ character] has an iPhone. Because I took liberties to tell a story I wanted to tell and make the movie look the way I wanted to make it look."

He also made a conscious choice to exclude beloved game characters like Leon S. Kennedy or Albert Wesker. "I intentionally didn’t want to put any of the characters in the games into the movie because what I don’t want to do is cannibalize all these things that the games do so well," he told Deadline. His goal was to celebrate the games' tone without stepping on their narrative toes.

Key confirmed details for the film include:
* **Director:** [Zach Cregger](/movies/zach-cregger-teases-weapons-prequel-gladys-says-script-is-in-progress/)
* **Lead Cast:** Austin Abrams as Bryan
* **Supporting Cast:** Zach Cherry as Dave, Kali Reis as Pauline, Paul Walter Hauser as Carl
* **Release Date:** September 18, 2026
* **Setting:** Modern-day Raccoon City during a viral outbreak

## Why a New Approach to Resident Evil?

Cregger, a self-professed fan of the games, framed his adaptation as a "love letter" focused on replicating the series' distinct atmosphere of dread rather than its plot points. He emphasized that 'Resident Evil' is survival horror, not survival action.

"He’s not a badass going around and karate kicking these zombies," Cregger said of Abrams' character.

His star, Austin Abrams, expressed full trust in the director's vision. "I just loved working with him so much," Abrams told Deadline. "I knew that even though it was *Resident Evil*, I was excited and not nervous because he loved *Resident Evil*."

## How Will Fans React?

Cregger is fully aware that his creative choices may not satisfy every fan. He directly addressed potential criticism during the [Comic-Con](/movies/san-diego-comic-con-2026-a-celebration-of-comics-or-a-digital-pop-culture/) panel.

"If the barrier of entry is whether I do a one-for-one port, then I guess in that regard, I have failed," he stated. "But I think the movie is stronger for it." He stands by the aesthetic decision, adding, "I think it looks really f---ing cool to have a horror movie set in the snow. I don’t see enough of those."

The film represents a significant bet for Sony, with a reported budget of $80 million. It aims to reboot the film franchise by returning to the tense, atmospheric roots of the games, distancing itself from the more action-oriented previous film series.

## What's Next for the Film?

With its release date locked for September 18, 2026, the film is in the final stages of its marketing campaign. Promotional efforts, including the panel at Comic-Con and trailer releases, are building awareness ahead of its theatrical debut. No further production milestones are required before the film opens nationwide.
