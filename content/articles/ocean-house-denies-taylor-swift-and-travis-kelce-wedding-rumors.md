## How We Got Here

Taylor Swift and Travis Kelce exchanged vows on July 3, according to reports, though the ceremony was kept private and sparked widespread speculation. Weeks before the nuptials, Page Six cited sources who claimed that Swift and Kelce wanted to get married at Ocean House in Watch Hill on June 13. At the time, viral rumors spread that the "Fate of Ophelia" songstress had reportedly paid another bride a large sum of money to surrender the wedding venue and date — and that the bride had agreed.

However, those claims were quickly debunked. Stephanie Leavitt, Area Director of Sales & Marketing for Ocean House, told TMZ the resort would never permit one couple to buy another couple's reserved date. She emphasized that when clients and their families choose Ocean House as their wedding venue, it is an agreement and commitment between the team and that family. "Ocean House would not and is not allowing another party or entity to buy a wedding group out of a contracted wedding date," she said.

## The Other Side

Despite the denials, speculation intensified. In June, a large tent was seen being erected next to Swift's Watch Hill estate, leading some fans to assume it might be for her wedding. But The Associated Press reported the setup was for a different event. Wedding planner Nicole Simeral clarified, "Next weekend, there'll be another tent just like this."

Taylor Swift and Travis Kelce celebrated their wedding at Madison Square Garden, not at Ocean House. The couple had sparked rumors they would tie the knot before the big day at Madison Square Garden, with several wedding vendors sharing posts that hinted at Swift and Kelce's nuptials before the July Fourth weekend.

## The Reaction

Wedding designer Rishi Patel wrote on Instagram Stories: "Today, I asked 23 of my colleagues if when they started in the event industry (span of 7 to 45 years) … they would have ever dreamed or imagined they would be part of what we created today … Unanimously the answer was no. That, in itself, means more than I can ever explain. I can'" — a post that, while vague, fueled further speculation.

Samantha Roberts, who owns a social media agency, shared a similar sentiment: "15 years ago I came to New York because I loved events. I dreamed that one day I'd work events like the one I did tonight," she wrote. No names were mentioned, but the implication was clear.

Knicks shooting guard Josh Hart refuted rumors that the team’s starting five were invited to the wedding, calling the reports "fake news."

## What Was Said

Stephanie Leavitt, Area Director of Sales & Marketing for Ocean House, not only denied the buyout claims but reaffirmed the venue’s values: "We appreciate the interest in our brides and grooms yet allow them to confirm their wedding location with who they choose."

The Associated Press, a wire service known for fact-based reporting, explicitly stated the tent was not for a Swift-Kelce wedding. Ocean House, a luxury resort with a reputation to protect, framed the idea of a date buyout as a violation of their core policies.

Taylor Swift and their representatives have denied this, and The Screen Report has not independently verified it — it remains unconfirmed.

The timeline is murky. Swift cheered for the New York Knicks during Game 4 of the NBA Finals in June, further fueling public appearances that fed speculation. But no statement from Swift or Kelce has confirmed a wedding took place — only the denials from third parties and the absence of evidence where proof would likely exist.

The story of their supposed nuptials now rests on a contradiction: viral hints from insiders versus flat denials from institutions. The venue says no. The wire service says no. The couple hasn’t said yes.
