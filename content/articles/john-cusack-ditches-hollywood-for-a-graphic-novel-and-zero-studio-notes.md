John Cusack, 60, once a leading man in Hollywood’s golden indie era, now finds his freedom in panels and ink. At San Diego Comic-Con on Friday, he didn’t promote a sequel or tease a streaming drop. Instead, he introduced *Momo* — his first graphic novel — as a quiet act of rebellion against the modern film machine.

The actor, known for *Being John Malkovich*, *Grosse Pointe Blank*, and *High Fidelity*, said he’s stepped back from mainstream Hollywood not out of retreat, but by design. He wanted to tell a story without selling it to a boardroom first.

“I didn’t want to negotiate telling a story,” Cusack told *Variety* at the convention.

## What Was Said

Cusack didn’t mince words about why he chose the graphic novel format. “I always thought [graphic novels] were the closest things to film, really,” he said. “You’re picking out shots, they’re so cinematic.”

For *Momo*, he wrote the story himself and teamed up with Ignacio Noé. Together, they built a world with no outside interference. “Nobody edited anything I wrote or that he drew,” Cusack said. “So it’s really a piece of work that was just done by two people. It was just me and Ignacio’s story, and that doesn’t happen today.” This was just like, I wrote it, and I said, ‘I like the story,’ and then I got this beautiful, wonderful artist, Ignacio Noé, to do the illustrations and so nobody

He called it an anti-algorithm thing.” "There’s no bull, and there’s no committee. There’s no computer up in Seattle that’s poll testing it," No focus groups. No data-driven tweaks. “There’s no bull, and there’s no committee.

He even called the project a “brand-buster” in a conversation with *Forbes* ahead of his panel — a jab at the formulaic nature of modern entertainment.

## How We Got Here

*Momo* is set in 1972 and follows two fugitives on a road trip to recover an ancient artifact — all at the request of TV legend Jackie Gleason. The plot blends heist, sci-fi, and historical fiction, with a twist Cusack claims is rooted in truth. “It’s based on a true story,” he told *Forbes*. “It’s all in recently declassified files that I managed to obtain.”

Cusack has been vocal about Hollywood’s shift for years. Back in 2014, he told *The Guardian* about his friend Joe Roth, who ran Disney until 2000. I got to make *High Fidelity* and *Grosse Pointe Blank*. Spike Lee got to make *Summer of Sam*. Wes Anderson got to make *Rushmore*.”

“That memory of film,” he said, “is gone.”

Now, he argues, it’s “six for them with a committee cutting the film who weren’t part of making it and maybe one for you.”

His recent film work reflects the pivot. Selective, yes — but not idle.

Still, *Momo* feels like a statement.

And it might not be a one-off. Cusack hinted to *Forbes* that he’s already working on a second book with Noé. No details were confirmed — only that the collaboration continues.

The graphic novel marks a full-circle moment for an actor who began in the 1980s with a breakout role in John Hughes’ *Sixteen Candles*, then rose through *Say Anything*, *Con Air*, and *High Fidelity* as a voice of offbeat sincerity. Now, he’s reclaiming that voice — just not on a soundstage.
