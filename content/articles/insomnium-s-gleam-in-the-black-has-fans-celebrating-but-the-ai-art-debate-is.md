The release of Insomnium's new single "Gleam In The Black" sent the melodic death metal community into a familiar state of rapture. For a band known for its consistency and emotional depth, the track is being hailed as another masterclass, a powerful preview of the upcoming album "Netherworlds." Yet, within the chorus of praise, a new and modern point of tension has emerged: the band's use of AI-generated imagery in the music video, creating a rare fissure in an otherwise unified fan response.

## A Triumphant Return to Form

The dominant reaction is one of unbridled enthusiasm. Fans are connecting the new track to the band's celebrated legacy, hearing echoes of beloved eras. The replies did not disappoint — one user wrote, "The more oldschool you bring back your sound the better." This sentiment was echoed by another viewer who felt a specific resonance, saying, "Fantastic Song! I'm getting big 'Across the Dark' vibes from this one. Can't wait for the full album."

For many, this release is further proof that Insomnium is operating at an historic peak. One fan posted a declaration that captured the prevailing awe: "We are witnessing one of the greatest metal bands of all time IN THEIR PRIME. This run they've been on since 1st album is unbelievable." This sense of unwavering quality is a common thread, with another supporter stating, "I say it once, and ill say it again."

The excitement has translated directly into feverish anticipation for "Netherworlds." One listener's comment, written in German, summed up the high expectations: "Wenn die anderen Lieder nur halb so gut sind wie die ersten beiden, ist es für mich jetzt schon das Album des Jahres." (If the other songs are only half as good as the first two, it's already my album of the year.) This is the overwhelming mood: a band at the height of its powers, delivering exactly what its audience craves.

## The AI Art Elephant in the Room

Amidst this celebration, however, a distinct and thoughtful criticism has surfaced, focused not on the music but on its visual accompaniment. The same fan who praised the "Across the Dark" vibes appended a heartfelt plea to their post: "(Just please don't use AI in your music videos)." This wasn't an isolated concern. Another fan expressed a deeper conflict, writing, "I love this band. But seeing artists using AI is juste the most conflicting thing to witness."

These comments highlight a growing tension in creative communities. For fans who deeply respect Insomnium as consummate artists, the choice to employ AI-generated art—a technology often criticized for its ethical implications and impact on human artists—feels like a puzzling misstep. It's a critique born of admiration, not malice, creating a discordant note in the reception. A separate, more aesthetic critique of the video was also offered, with one viewer noting, "The film jitters are a bit much in my opinion, but the rest of the video is good. Insomnium killing it like usual."

## Why This Song Resonates

The reason the music itself is landing so powerfully is no mystery. In an official statement, vocalist and bassist Niilo Sevänen framed the song's intent, calling it a faster track that "should get the crowd moving when played live." He pinpointed its core appeal, stating, "It definitely carries a classic Insomnium spirit. The lyrics tell a dark and mysterious love story, capturing the mood and atmosphere of the entire album." This description aligns perfectly with fan reactions that praise the song's "gothic vibe," its "magic sound," and how the music "always tells a story." It's a deliberate return to a foundational energy that the fanbase cherishes.

The conversation around "Gleam In The Black" is thus split into two clear channels. The primary, roaring channel is one of pure acclaim for a band reaffirming its legendary status. The secondary, quieter channel is a sober discussion about the ethics of modern creative tools, a debate that has now landed on Insomnium's doorstep. As the release of "Netherworlds" approaches, the music is poised to unite everyone once more. But this initial glimpse suggests that even a band as revered as Insomnium isn't immune to the complex debates defining art in the digital age.
