The creators of Avatar: The Last Airbender have detailed the ideas and character concepts that were ultimately cut from the new animated film, according to an interview with Variety. Michael Dante DiMartino and Bryan Konietzko, alongside the film's director Lauren Montgomery, spoke about the development process for "Avatar [Aang: The Last Airbender](/movies/avatar-aang-the-last-airbender-revives-the-franchise-with-breathtaking/)" following its release on Paramount+.

This marks the first new on-screen adventure for the original Team Avatar characters in years. The film is a direct extension of the world DiMartino and Konietzko built, produced through their Avatar Studios at Nickelodeon.

## What Ideas Were Left Out?

The creative team had several concepts that evolved or were removed during production. In their discussion with Variety, they shared specific details about the film's development.

* **An Alternate Family Storyline:** The creators originally considered a plot centered on Aang and Katara about to start their family, but moved away from it later in development.
* **Expanded Action for Tagah:** Director Lauren Montgomery noted that while the film is packed with action, extensive ideas for the new character Tagah's unique fighting style were discussed but not all made it into the final cut.
* **A Focus on Aang's Central Conflict:** The team settled on exploring Aang's lingering pain as "the last Airbender" as the core emotional story, deeming it the most personal narrative left to tell for the character.

Montgomery expressed a slight regret about the action that was trimmed, telling Variety, "We had so many awesome conversations about [Tagah's] fighting style, and we didn't even get to get all of it in there. There were so many things we discussed, and I wish we could have fit it, but ultimately, there was a lot of story to be told."

## What Is the New Film About?

"Avatar Aang: The Last Airbender" is an animated feature film directed by Lauren Montgomery. It features a voice cast including Eric Nam as Aang, Dave Bautista as Tagah, Jessica Matten as Katara, Román Zaragoza as Sokka, Steven Yeun as Zuko, and Dionne Quan as Toph.

The story is set 13 years after the defeat of Fire Lord Ozai. Aang, now a young adult and well-established as the Avatar, is still haunted by being the last of his culture. His journey leads him to discover an ancient airbender named Tagah and a lost Avatar named Sonam (voiced by Freida Pinto), who may hold the key to saving the Air Nomads from extinction. The film is rated PG and has a runtime of 99 minutes.

## Why Return to the Original Characters?

DiMartino and Konietzko explained their reasoning for revisiting Aang, Katara, Sokka, Toph, and Zuko. DiMartino stated that focusing on the "most iconic and most beloved characters" made sense for the studio's first feature film. Aging them up to young adulthood provided a fresh way to re-enter their world.

Konietzko was initially hesitant, wanting to let the original characters "have some peace and privacy." However, he found the concept compelling once DiMartino pitched the idea of exploring them at this specific age. "He's also the age that I was when Mike and I created 'Avatar,'" Konietzko said, relating Aang's journey to his own early career experiences.

## How Can You Watch It?

"Avatar Aang: The Last Airbender" is a streaming-original film released exclusively on Paramount+. It began streaming globally on July 25, 2026. As of July 2026, the film is available to subscribers on that platform.

## What's Next for Avatar Studios?

The release of this film represents the first major project from Avatar Studios, which DiMartino and Konietzko returned to Nickelodeon to launch. The studio's stated priority is to expand the "Avatar" universe through both television and film. While this movie focuses on the original characters, the creators have expressed interest in exploring other parts of the Avatar world in future projects. No specific follow-up films or series have been confirmed at this time.
