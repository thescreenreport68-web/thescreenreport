Luke Bracey discussed his portrayal of Charles Ingalls in Netflix's new adaptation of "[Little House on the Prairie](/tv/little-house-on-the-prairie-the-new-series-that-has-fans-in-a-chokehold-over/)," emphasizing a more human and imperfect character. The series, which premiered recently, has been renewed for a second season and topped Netflix's English-language TV ratings in its second week.

Bracey, an Australian actor known for films like *Hacksaw Ridge* and *Point Break*, landed the role of the pioneer patriarch. In an interview with *The Hollywood Reporter*, he detailed his approach to a character often idealized in Laura Ingalls Wilder's books.

## What is Luke Bracey's take on Charles Ingalls?

Bracey and the creative team deliberately chose not to depict Charles Ingalls as a flawless hero. "We didn’t want him to be perfect," Bracey told THR. "We wanted him to be a human and make some mistakes. But he also is a good man. I think those two things live together in Pa."

His interpretation centers on Charles's relentless optimism, which borders on naiveté. "He’s not just a glass half-full guy. He’s a cup overfloweth guy," Bracey said. This trait leads the family to Kansas on the promise of cheap land, only to face harsh realities, broken promises, and the need to move again by the season's end. The actor sees this optimism as both a strength and a flaw Pa must learn to manage.

## How is the new 'Little House' different?

The 2026 Netflix adaptation brings a contemporary perspective to the classic story. Spearheaded by showrunner Rebecca Sonnenshine, the series features a predominantly female creative team, which sources say brings a more feminist lens. Key changes from the original narrative include:

- A more prominent and empathetic portrayal of the Osage Nation, whose land the Ingalls family settles on
- The addition of new characters like Lacey Aubert, a French woman who runs a gambling room and challenges gender norms
- A Black doctor, Dr. Tann, played by Jocko Sims
- A deeper exploration of male characters' mental health, particularly trauma from the Civil War

The first season adapts the third book in Wilder's series, following the family's move from Wisconsin to Kansas in the 1870s.

## Why did the show resonate with viewers?

The series found a significant audience quickly. According to *Deadline*, "Little House on the Prairie" jumped to No. This strong performance contributed to Netflix's decision to renew the show for a second season.

For Bracey, the appeal lay in the show's fundamental decency. "Something that appealed to me about the whole show is that it’s a really high-quality production about nice people," he said. "That’s kind of rare. I feel privileged to have been able to be part of something that tells a nice story about people trying to be good."

## What's next for Charles Ingalls in Season 2?

Charles will retain his core optimism but become more discerning. "In season two, we find him trying to take the measure of people a little bit more," he explained. "He absolutely always thinks the best of people, but he’s not going to be as easily manipulated. He’s a little bit more streetwise."

The family's journey is set to continue, with the first season ending as they pack up to leave Kansas, headed for the iconic setting of Walnut Grove, Minnesota.

## The Original Series on Streaming

The original 'Little House on the Prairie' television series, which first aired in 1974, was created by Ed Friendly and Laura Ingalls Wilder. According to IMDb, it holds a 7.5/10 rating and is rated TV-PG. The series won 4 Primetime Emmys and garnered 17 wins and 44 nominations total.

As of July 2026, the 1974 series is available to stream in the US on Amazon Prime Video, Peacock Premium, Philo, and Peacock Premium Plus. Its cast included Melissa Gilbert as Laura Ingalls and featured actors like Dean Butler, Katherine MacGregor, and a young Shannen Doherty.
