Both products, which nod to the stripped-down, high-impact guitar tone that has defined his career, are available for purchase now.

The announcement arrives during a busy period for Armstrong, who is also promoting the upcoming Green Day film *Nimrods* and performing with his side-project band, The Coverups. The new gear directly translates the sonic cornerstones of Armstrong's three-decade career into instruments fans can own.

## What Are the New Products?

The two new items are designed to capture the essence of Armstrong's iconic sound.

* **Epiphone Billie Joe Armstrong Les Paul Junior (Radiant Red):** A limited-edition guitar with a single-cutaway mahogany body, a single Epiphone P-90 PRO pickup, and a deliberately simple control layout of master volume and tone knobs. It retails for $649.
* **Marshall 1959BJA Artist Signature Amplifier:** Marshall's first artist signature amp model in 14 years. Based on the handwired 1959HW platform, it includes a custom "Dookie Mod" inspired by the guitar tone developed for Green Day's 1994 album. It features a baby-blue covering as a tribute to Armstrong's early guitar, "Blue."

"I’m so overjoyed to have my own signature Marshall amp," Armstrong said in a statement. "These amps have been a part of my musical life, from my heroes down to little old me. Turn it the f— up!"

## The Gear's Connection to Green Day's History

Both pieces of equipment are deeply rooted in Armstrong's musical journey. The Epiphone model is a faithful recreation of the Les Paul Junior style he has favored for decades, built around what the company calls the "straightforward formula that has always defined great punk rock: one pickup, two knobs, and absolutely no distractions." It includes a reproduction of his signature on the headstock and comes with a custom black hardshell case featuring a leopard-print interior.

The Marshall amplifier's design and modifications are even more specific. The included "Dookie Mod" is a tonal circuit inspired by the work he did with producer Rob Cavallo on Green Day's multi-platinum breakthrough album, *Dookie*, which cemented the band's signature guitar sound.

## A Busy Time for Armstrong and Green Day

This gear launch is not happening in a vacuum. Earlier in July, Green Day announced the soundtrack for their upcoming coming-of-age comedy film, *Nimrods*. The 22-track collection is due on July 31 and includes career-spanning favorites, four previously unreleased live recordings, and a new song titled "I’m Never Gonna R.I.P." The film itself, in which the band appears as themselves, is scheduled for release in August.

Armstrong has also been active on stage with his covers band, The Coverups. At recent shows, he was joined by Iron Maiden frontman Bruce Dickinson for a cover of David Bowie's "All The Young Dudes," and the group performed Nirvana's "Drain You." Green Day's most recent studio album, *Saviors*, was released in January 2024, continuing the band's long-running presence on the charts.

## Where to Find the New Signature Gear

The limited-edition Epiphone Billie Joe Armstrong Les Paul Junior is available worldwide through authorized Epiphone dealers, Gibson Garage locations in Nashville and London, and on the Epiphone website. For fans and guitarists, these releases offer a tangible piece of punk rock history, built to the specifications of one of the genre's most enduring figures.

## The 'Dookie Mod' and a Producer's Legacy

The ultimate distillation of producer Rob Cavallo's rock sound perfectly partnered with the Green Day guitarist's chunky power chords, producing one of the most familiar and beloved tones in modern history. This tonal circuit is a key feature of the handwired 1959HW-based amp, translating that specific studio sound into a playable amplifier.
